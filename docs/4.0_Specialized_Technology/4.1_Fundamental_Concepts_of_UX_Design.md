  # 4.1 Fundamental Concepts of UX Design
	
First and foremost, user experience (UX) design is a user-centered approach that focus on how people interact with digital system. UX design focuses on understanding what users need, how they use a system, and the problems they face while using it. The purpose is to make the system easier to use and help users complete their tasks without confusion. UX design is not only about making a system look attractive, but also about making sure users can easily find information, navigate between pages, and complete their tasks efficiently.

Furthermore. Another important concept in UX design is usability by this it refer to how easily users can complete tasks effectively and efficiently. Accessibility focuses on making digital interfaces usable by people with different abilities. Information architecture involves organizing information logically so users can locate relevant content without unnecessary navigation. Visual hierarchy helps users recognize important information through the arrangement of headings, colours, spacing, and other interface elements.

Other than that, UX design generally follows an iterative process. Designers first identify user problems and understand their needs. Next, they organize information and propose interface improvements based on those findings. The proposed design can then be evaluated through user feedback or usability testing. This process allows designers to identify weaknesses and refine the interface before implementation.

Overall, in the context of UiTM uFuture, these concepts can be applied to improve how students manage their academic activities. For example, a centralized Academic Dashboard could display upcoming assignment deadlines and attendance reminders in one location. Clear visual priority indicators could help students identify urgent tasks, while consistent navigation could reduce the effort required to move between the My Courses, Assignment, and Attendance pages.
